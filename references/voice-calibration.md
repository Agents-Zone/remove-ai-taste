# Voice Calibration

Use this reference only when the user supplies or explicitly approves samples for voice matching.

## Boundaries

- Learn repeated expression habits, not identity.
- Do not claim that the sampled author wrote the result.
- Do not copy distinctive phrases, titles, metaphors, jokes, or paragraph sequences.
- Do not transfer facts, experiences, clients, cases, institutions, or private details from the samples.
- Do not fetch private or third-party samples without permission.
- Preserve the target text's facts and genre when a sample conflicts with them.

## Build The Profile

Read the sample as a whole, then record only traits that recur.

1. **Sentence length**: typical range and how short and long sentences alternate.
2. **Vocabulary level**: conversational, neutral, technical, academic, or mixed.
3. **Paragraph openings**: direct claim, scene, observation, question, chronology, or evidence.
4. **Punctuation**: frequency and function of commas, colons, dashes, parentheses, and quotation marks.
5. **Transitions**: explicit connectors, implied links, conditional movement, or chronology.
6. **Claim density**: number of judgments per paragraph and how evidence is placed.
7. **Tone**: first person, direct address, humor, skepticism, certainty, and emotional range.

Record counterexamples separately. An isolated flourish is not a stable voice trait.

## Match And Verify

After rewriting:

- Check the seven dimensions against the profile.
- Check that counterexamples did not become repeated habits.
- Search for copied distinctive phrases.
- Confirm that no sample facts entered the target text.
- Prefer the target genre when voice and genre conflict.

Describe the result as matching a voice profile. Do not describe it as impersonation.
